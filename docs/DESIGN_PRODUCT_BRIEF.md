# THURSDAY POST

## Design & Product Brief for Claude Design

### Core instruction

Use the existing **Claude Design broadsheet newspaper template as the foundation**.

Do **not** reinvent the layout into a magazine, modern news app, dashboard, card grid, blog, or generic AI website.

We already like the basic broadsheet direction.

The task is to turn that template into the identity and editorial system for:

# THURSDAY POST

An independent Australian horse-racing publication built around researched stories, industry intelligence, personalities, racing culture, reader information, verified imagery and strong editorial judgement.

The finished product should immediately feel like a **real newspaper**.

Think:

* established racing newspaper
* traditional broadsheet authority
* contemporary enough to work digitally
* confident
* intelligent
* slightly irreverent when appropriate
* visually restrained
* not corporate
* not glossy
* not tabloid
* definitely not an AI-tech product

The reader should think:

**“This is a racing newspaper.”**

Not:

**“This is a website generated by AI.”**

---

# 1. BRAND

## Publication name

**Thursday Post**

The words should have genuine masthead presence.

The masthead should be one of the strongest visual elements on the page.

Preferred treatment:

**THURSDAY POST**

or

**The Thursday Post**

Test both visually, but favour the version that looks most like an established newspaper title.

Do not make the masthead overly modern or minimalist.

It should feel as though the newspaper could have existed for decades even though the publication itself is new.

---

# 2. LOGO

The publication logo is:

## A horse at the post.

This should become a recognisable publication mark.

The concept is a racehorse at or approaching the starting/post position, simplified into a strong newspaper-style emblem.

It should not look like:

* a betting company
* a racing club
* a cartoon horse
* a sports-team mascot
* a glossy corporate logo
* an AI-generated fantasy horse

It should feel more like an old newspaper mark, racing engraving, seal or editorial emblem that has been simplified enough to work digitally.

The horse mark can sit:

* beside the masthead
* between elements of the masthead
* above the title in some applications
* by itself as the favicon/publication icon

The logo should reproduce well in black and white.

---

# 3. OVERALL VISUAL LANGUAGE

Preserve the **broadsheet newspaper DNA** of the template.

Strong use of:

* columns
* rules
* headlines
* subheads
* captions
* pull quotes
* small metadata
* section labels
* photographs
* white space
* editorial hierarchy

Avoid excessive rounded cards.

Avoid the visual language of:

* SaaS dashboards
* social-media feeds
* Substack clones
* betting apps
* racing-form apps
* generic news aggregators

Stories should feel physically placed onto a newspaper page rather than poured into interchangeable website components.

There should be some controlled visual imperfection and asymmetry.

A newspaper page does not need every article to occupy an identical rectangle.

---

# 4. COLOUR

The design should remain predominantly newspaper-neutral.

Base palette:

* warm newspaper white / off-white
* black
* charcoal
* muted grey

Use **one restrained publication accent colour** where required for:

* section identifiers
* rules
* small labels
* links
* selected editorial elements

Do not turn the page into a brightly coloured racing website.

Photography and headlines should carry most of the visual energy.

The design must still look excellent when effectively monochrome.

---

# 5. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography is extremely important.

The publication should combine:

### Editorial serif

For:

* masthead
* major headlines
* feature headlines
* article body copy

### Clean sans serif

For:

* metadata
* dates
* author information
* navigation
* captions
* labels
* supporting UI

Major stories should be capable of having **very large editorial headlines**.

Do not make every headline the same size.

Hierarchy matters.

A front page should clearly communicate:

1. the story everyone should read
2. the next two or three major stories
3. secondary industry news
4. smaller observations and briefs

before the reader has read a single word.

---

# 6. THE FRONT PAGE

The front page should look like an actual edition of a newspaper.

At the top:

**Thursday Post masthead**

Horse-at-the-post logo.

Edition/date information.

Potential small line describing the publication:

**Australian Racing — News, People & Industry**

Do not overfill the masthead.

Below this should be a strong multi-column broadsheet structure.

The page needs:

### One dominant lead story

Large headline.

Strong image when an appropriate verified image exists.

Deck/subheadline.

Short opening copy.

Clear continuation/read-more treatment.

### Secondary stories

Two or three important supporting stories with varying visual weight.

### Smaller pieces

Briefs, observations, updates and smaller stories can occupy narrower columns.

Do not create a homepage where twelve articles have identical cards.

---

# 7. EDITORIAL CHARACTER

Thursday Post should support two things simultaneously.

## We like racing.

The publication should recognise and report:

* good racing
* good people
* regional racing
* innovation
* community contribution
* trainers
* jockeys
* owners
* breeders
* clubs
* stable staff
* administrators
* bookmakers
* syndicators
* racing businesses
* racing culture

The default stance toward the industry itself is not cynical.

But:

## We are not the industry's PR department.

Thursday Post must also comfortably publish stories involving:

* questionable behaviour
* disputes
* governance problems
* integrity matters
* regulatory issues
* welfare issues
* financial problems
* contradictions
* unusual behaviour
* controversial personalities
* uncomfortable facts

The design therefore needs enough editorial authority to carry both a celebratory racing story and a serious investigative story without changing personality.

Internally, stories may be assessed as:

**A — positive / constructive**

**B — adverse / dubious**

**N — neutral**

This classification is an editorial measurement tool, not a quota that should be displayed prominently to readers.

The broader editorial goal is:

**Get readers into the goss without looking like gossip.**

That sentence is important.

Thursday Post should often know the interesting thing people are talking about, but it earns the right to publish it through verification, sourcing and editorial restraint.

---

# 8. WRITERS / EDITORIAL IDENTITIES

Public stories can be attributed to editorial agents.

Examples:

**Agent 1**
**Agent 2**
**Agent 3**
**Agent 5**

Internally these are **PE Agents**.

Do not display “PE” publicly.

The public-facing author should simply appear as:

**By Agent 3**

for example.

Treat these exactly as newspaper bylines.

Do not use:

* robot icons
* AI sparkles
* chatbot graphics
* “AI generated” visual styling

The technology behind the publication should remain largely invisible.

The newspaper is the product.

---

# 9. IMAGES

Photography is important, but image integrity is part of the publication.

Every image entering the publication system should support verification of:

* source
* subject
* horse/person/event represented
* date where relevant
* whether the image matches the story
* previous use
* potential manipulation
* potential AI generation
* publication rights

The public design should support conventional newspaper captions.

Example:

**Caption describing the photograph. PHOTO: Source/Photographer**

Do not overwhelm the reader with technical verification information.

However, the system should allow editorial staff to know that imagery has been checked.

AI imagery must never be presented in a way that could mislead the reader into believing it is documentary photography.

---

# 10. STORY PRESENTATION

Individual story pages should retain the newspaper character.

Structure:

Section label

Large headline

Deck / subheadline

Byline

Publication date and update date

Hero image where appropriate

Image caption/source

Article body

Pull quotes

Supporting photographs

Related coverage

Sources or editorial notes where appropriate

Correction/update information where applicable

Reader contact/tip information near the bottom

Articles should be pleasant to read at length.

Body-column width should therefore remain newspaper/editorial rather than stretching across an entire desktop monitor.

---

# 11. CORRECTIONS

Thursday Post needs a visible:

## Corrections & Updates

area.

This is important to the credibility of the newspaper.

A correction should clearly state:

* what Thursday Post originally reported
* what was incorrect
* the correct information
* which story/edition was affected
* when the correction was made

An **update** is not automatically a **correction**.

The design should distinguish the two.

Corrections should not be hidden.

A corrected article should display a restrained but visible correction notice.

---

# 12. RIGHT OF REPLY

For materially adverse stories, the editorial system needs room to record or display responses from relevant people or organisations.

Possible article treatment:

### Response

or

### Right of Reply

This should visually form part of the journalism rather than appearing like a comments section.

---

# 13. READER INFORMATION & EMAIL

Readers are themselves one of Thursday Post's information sources.

This is an important part of the publication.

We want readers to contact the newspaper with:

* things they have seen
* things they have heard
* racing rumours worth investigating
* corrections
* photographs
* tips
* local racing information
* questions
* feedback
* possible stories
* anecdotal information

The newspaper does **not** automatically publish reader claims.

Reader information becomes another lead for the publication to investigate and verify.

Create a recurring editorial callout such as:

## Heard Something?

**Tell the Thursday Post.**

Seen something at the track? Heard something worth checking? Got a correction, photo, tip or story we should investigate?

**Email the newsroom.**

This can appear:

* toward the bottom of stories
* in the newspaper footer
* periodically on the front page
* in its own reader-feedback area

It should feel like the traditional relationship between a newspaper and its readers.

Not like a generic website contact form.

---

# 14. READER MAIL

Provide an editorial treatment for reader correspondence.

Possible working title:

## Post Box

This can contain:

* letters
* comments
* short reader observations
* questions
* selected feedback

It should visually resemble an established newspaper letters section.

Reader submissions can also feed the newspaper's research system behind the scenes.

---

# 15. POSSIBLE PAPER STRUCTURE

The exact section names can evolve.

Do not overengineer the navigation in the first design.

The publication should be capable of containing areas such as:

### Front Page

Major stories across racing.

### Racing

The sport itself.

### Industry

Business, administration, regulation, ownership, breeding and the machinery around racing.

### People

Jockeys, trainers, owners, administrators and other racing characters.

### Regional

Stories outside the obvious metropolitan racing bubble.

### Inside Racing

The conversations, unusual developments and stories circulating within the industry that deserve closer examination.

### Opinion / Analysis

Clearly differentiated from straight reporting.

### Post Box

Reader correspondence and feedback.

### Corrections & Updates

The permanent corrections record.

These labels are a starting editorial structure rather than a requirement to turn the website into a giant menu.

---

# 16. STORY LABELS

The system should support restrained labels including:

BREAKING

EXCLUSIVE

ANALYSIS

OPINION

INVESTIGATION

UPDATE

CORRECTION

RIGHT OF REPLY

Do not overuse them.

If everything is “BREAKING” or “EXCLUSIVE”, they become meaningless.

---

# 17. TRUST & EVIDENCE

Thursday Post's authority comes from the difference between:

**something being interesting**

and

**something being true enough to publish.**

The interface should therefore allow stories to exist internally at different levels of confidence without exposing unfinished allegations publicly.

The public design should convey certainty only when the editorial process supports it.

Where appropriate, stories can explain:

* what is known
* what is alleged
* what remains unclear
* who has been contacted
* what evidence supports the story

This should feel like journalism, not a machine-generated confidence score.

---

# 18. EDITORIAL INTELLIGENCE

Behind Thursday Post is a much larger collection and research system.

Information can arrive from:

* racing websites
* news publications
* racing organisations
* official sources
* racing clubs
* social media
* public records
* historical material
* reader emails
* customer/reader feedback
* tips
* anecdotal reports
* previous Thursday Post stories
* corrections
* responses from people involved
* photographs
* other research

Research agents collect and connect this information.

Editorial agents decide what deserves to become a story.

This process should **not dominate the visual identity**.

Again:

Thursday Post should look like a newspaper, not an intelligence platform.

---

# 19. PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

The internal publication principle is:

# COLLECT. AGGREGATE. DISTRIBUTE.

We collect information.

We aggregate it into useful intelligence and journalism.

We distribute the resulting stories to readers.

This can influence the architecture but should not necessarily become a giant public slogan.

The newspaper itself remains the hero.

---

# 20. DIGITAL BROADSHEET BEHAVIOUR

Desktop should preserve the broadsheet experience wherever practical.

Use multiple columns and strong horizontal/vertical rules.

On tablet and mobile, intelligently reflow the same hierarchy rather than turning everything into identical cards.

The order of importance must survive responsive design.

For example:

A huge front-page story on desktop must still obviously be the lead story on mobile.

Do not let responsive behaviour flatten the editorial hierarchy.

---

# 21. NEWSPAPER DETAILS

Small details will make the design believable.

Include restrained treatments for:

* edition date
* issue number
* page/section references where useful
* author
* location/dateline where relevant
* image credits
* reading continuation
* update timestamp
* correction status
* quotations
* source attribution
* small section rules
* edition footer

Do not fake historical dates or pretend the newspaper has existed longer than it has.

The aesthetic can feel established without inventing history.

---

# 22. ADVERTISING

The layout should be capable of accommodating traditional newspaper advertising without the ads overwhelming journalism.

Ad spaces should feel native to a newspaper layout.

Potential sizes can include:

* narrow column advertising
* horizontal strip
* half-width panel
* occasional larger display advertisement

Clearly distinguish advertising from editorial content.

Use:

**ADVERTISEMENT**

where necessary.

Never blur sponsored material into journalism.

---

# 23. WHAT TO AVOID

Do not turn Thursday Post into:

* ESPN
* Racing.com
* a betting portal
* a form guide
* a social network
* a Substack page
* a generic WordPress publication
* an AI dashboard
* an analytics product
* a corporate racing website
* a clickbait tabloid

Avoid:

* excessive gradients
* glowing elements
* oversized rounded cards
* emoji
* AI iconography
* chatbot motifs
* bright tech colours
* unnecessary animations
* glassmorphism
* dashboard widgets
* infinite social-feed styling

Restraint is part of the brand.

---

# 24. THE FEELING WE ARE TRYING TO CREATE

Imagine somebody at a stable, racetrack, pub, office or kitchen table opening Thursday Post on Thursday morning.

Within seconds they should feel:

**There is something in here I didn't know.**

Then:

**These people know what is going on.**

Then:

**I trust them enough to keep reading.**

And occasionally:

**Bloody hell — I can't believe they've got that story.**

That is the product.

---

# 25. FIRST CLAUDE DESIGN PASS

For the first design pass, concentrate on visually perfecting:

1. Thursday Post masthead
2. horse-at-the-post logo position
3. front-page broadsheet hierarchy
4. lead story
5. secondary story hierarchy
6. newspaper typography
7. imagery and captions
8. Agent bylines
9. reader-tip/email callout
10. Corrections & Updates treatment
11. desktop broadsheet layout
12. intelligent mobile reflow

Do **not** spend the first pass building complicated secondary functionality.

First make the publication itself look right.

---

# FINAL DESIGN RULE

**Start with Claude's existing broadsheet template and refine it.**

Do not replace the core design language simply because another layout is technically cleaner.

Thursday Post should possess a stable visual identity that we can continue improving rather than receiving a completely different design every time the product changes.

The broadsheet is the foundation.

**Thursday Post is the newspaper.**

**The horse at the post is the mark.**

Everything else should serve the journalism.
